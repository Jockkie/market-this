# Use cases: common go-to-market patterns

Match by business type and symptom. Each case gives the pillar that is usually weakest, the play, the first cheap test and the metric. These are starting hypotheses; the diagnosis in Phase 2 always wins.

| ID | Business type | Typical symptom | Usually weakest pillar | Play | First test (2 to 4 weeks) | Metric |
|----|---------------|-----------------|------------------------|------|---------------------------|--------|
| GTM-01 | Local service (installer, cleaner, garage) | Only referrals, feast and famine | Channels | Google Business profile, reviews after every job, partnerships with adjacent trades | Ask every customer this month for a review; two partner referral deals | Inquiries per week |
| GTM-02 | Local service with full agenda | Busy but low margin | Pricing | Raise price for new customers, package the most requested job | New price for the next 10 quotes | Close rate and margin |
| GTM-03 | B2B service (consultant, agency, freelancer) | Vague "we do everything" | Customer and positioning | Niche down to one segment with one trigger; one case study | 30 personal messages to the segment with one specific offer | Conversations booked |
| GTM-04 | B2B service, proposals not closing | Many talks, few deals | Offer | Paid first step (audit, sprint), clear scope, guarantee | Offer the small first step to the next 5 prospects | Close rate |
| GTM-05 | Online course or coaching | Few sales after launch | Problem and message | Sell the transformation for one trigger moment; waiting list and pre-sale before building more | Five customer conversations, then a pre-sale page | Pre-sales |
| GTM-06 | Creator with audience, no revenue | Many followers, nobody buys | Offer | One paid product tied to the most asked question | Small paid workshop announced to the audience | Buyers per 1,000 followers |
| GTM-07 | E-commerce, own brand | Traffic but low conversion | Message and proof | Product page rewrite, reviews with photos, clear shipping and returns | Rewrite the top product page (Copywrite Me) | Conversion rate |
| GTM-08 | E-commerce, no traffic | Nice shop, no visitors | Channels | Marketplaces as a channel, creator seeding, one search intent per page | List the bestseller on one marketplace; send product to 10 small creators | Sessions and sales per channel |
| GTM-09 | SaaS or app, early | Sign-ups but no paying users | Activation (offer) | Shorten time to first value; onboarding that reaches the aha moment | Interview 5 users who dropped off; fix the first blocker | Activation rate |
| GTM-10 | SaaS, high price, self-serve | Visitors leave at pricing | Motion | Add sales contact or a demo for larger buyers | "Book a call" on the pricing page | Calls booked |
| GTM-11 | Restaurant, cafe, shop | Quiet weekdays | Offer and channels | A weekday reason to come (event, menu, loyalty), local partners | One weekday event promoted locally | Covers on that day |
| GTM-12 | Care or wellness practice | Waiting list or empty agenda by season | Market timing and channels | Referral partners (GP, physio), clear intake offer, local search | Two referral partner meetings | Intakes per week |
| GTM-13 | Events and workshops | Last-minute ticket sales | Proof and urgency (real) | Early-bird tied to a real limit, partners, past attendees | Early-bird window with a real cap | Tickets sold by week |
| GTM-14 | Membership or community | High churn | Retention (proof and offer) | Onboarding ritual, member wins, annual plan | Welcome call or ritual for new members | Month-2 retention |
| GTM-15 | Product for businesses via resellers | Resellers do not push it | Partner motion | Partner kit, margin, joint campaign | One co-marketing action with the best partner | Partner-sourced sales |
| GTM-16 | New idea, no product yet | "Is there a market?" | Market and problem | Customer conversations, smoke test page, pre-sale | 10 Mom Test conversations, then a waiting list page | Sign-ups or pre-sales |

## Patterns across cases

- The channel that works for the first 20 customers often stops working after that. Plan the second channel before the first saturates.
- Your first case study is worth more than any ad; treat the first customers as a way to create proof, not only revenue.
- Raise prices in small steps on new prospects first; existing customers later and with notice.
