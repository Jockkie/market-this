# Motions and channels

## Go-to-market motions

| Motion | Fits when | First move |
|--------|-----------|------------|
| Product-led | Low price, quick value, self-serve possible | Free trial or free tool, onboarding that reaches value in minutes |
| Sales-led | High price, several decision makers, custom work | Direct outreach to a short list, discovery calls, proposals |
| Community-led | The audience gathers around a topic you own | Host or join the place they meet, give before asking |
| Partner-led | Others already serve your ICP | Referral or reseller deals with two complementary businesses |
| Content-led | Buyers research before buying, long consideration | One pillar topic, one long format, repurposed |

Most small businesses start sales-led or partner-led for the first ten customers, even if they want to be product-led later.

## The 19 traction channels (Bullseye method)

1. Viral and referral loops
2. Public relations
3. Unconventional PR (stunts, giveaways)
4. Search engine marketing (ads)
5. Social and display ads
6. Offline ads (print, radio, outdoor)
7. Search engine optimization
8. Content marketing (blog, newsletter, video, podcast)
9. Email marketing
10. Engineering as marketing (free tools, calculators)
11. Targeting blogs and creators
12. Business development (partnerships)
13. Sales (direct outreach)
14. Affiliate programs
15. Existing platforms (marketplaces, app stores, directories)
16. Trade shows
17. Offline events and workshops
18. Speaking engagements
19. Community building

## Bullseye in five steps

1. **Outer ring:** brainstorm one concrete idea per channel for this business. Skip none on purpose; surprising channels often win.
2. **Middle ring:** pick the six most promising, judged on reach of the ICP, cost, time to first result and fit with the user's skills and values.
3. **Inner ring:** pick two.
4. **Cheap test per channel:** define the test, the budget in money and hours, the duration (two to four weeks) and the success threshold (for example: cost per lead under X, or three conversations booked).
5. **Decide:** keep, adjust or kill after the test. Add a new channel only when one works.

## Live guideline check (mandatory)

Platforms change their rules and what they reward constantly. Before advising on a specific channel, search the web for the current state, for example:
- "<platform> algorithm <current year>", "<platform> best practices <current month and year>"
- official sources first: the platform's own creator or business help center, then recent reputable analyses
- format specs: lengths, aspect ratios, character limits, posting frequency, what is penalised (links, engagement bait, AI-labeled content rules)

Write the findings into the dossier under `channel_guidelines` with the date, source and confidence. Official platform sources are high confidence; recent reputable third-party analyses are medium; older or anonymous posts are low. If no official platform source is reachable, say so, cap confidence at medium, and do not quote any frequency, length or format number you did not actually read in a source. Record only what was verified. A test proposal (for example how often to post) must stay inside a range a source gave and be labelled "my proposal"; if no source gives a frequency, propose the test without a number and let the user's capacity set it. Say plainly when sources disagree. If you cannot search, tell the user the advice may be outdated and what to check.

## Distribution, not only content

For every channel, write down how a piece actually reaches people: owned (list, site), rented (social platforms), earned (press, creators, word of mouth), paid. Content without a distribution route is a draft, not marketing.
