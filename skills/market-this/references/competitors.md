# Competitors and alternatives

Competitors are not only similar businesses. The real alternatives are whatever the customer would otherwise do: another provider, a different kind of solution, doing it themselves, hiring someone, or doing nothing.

## How to research

1. List three to five alternatives from the interview (topic 5) and from customer conversations.
2. For each, collect public information only: website, offer, pricing page, positioning line, main promise, call to action, reviews (what customers praise and complain about), channels they are visible in.
3. Sources: the user, a web search, or Monid (quote and cap first, see `tools.md`).
4. **Cite every claim to a source and date. Flag low-confidence claims.** Pricing pages and promises change; never present an old snapshot as current.

## The matrix

Fill `templates/competitor-matrix.md`. Then look for:
- **Gaps:** what customers complain about across alternatives that the user does well.
- **Crowded claims:** promises everyone makes ("quality", "personal", "fast"). These cannot be the positioning.
- **Unserved segments:** customers the alternatives ignore or serve badly.
- **Price frame:** where the user's price sits, and whether it signals the right thing.

## From matrix to positioning

Feed the gaps and unserved segments into the positioning template. The unique attributes must be things the alternatives cannot credibly claim, with proof.

## Respect

Compare facts, never disparage. Do not copy competitors' texts, visuals or names into the user's marketing.
