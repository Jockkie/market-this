# Customer conversations

Real conversations beat any simulation or survey. This file helps the user run them without fooling themselves. Based on the three rules of *The Mom Test* by Rob Fitzpatrick.

## The three rules

1. Talk about their life, not your idea.
2. Ask about specifics in the past, not generics or opinions about the future.
3. Talk less, listen more.

Compliments, hypothetical promises ("I would definitely buy that") and feature wishes are not data. Past behaviour, money and time already spent are.

## Script: five to ten conversations of fifteen minutes

**Opening:** "I'm trying to understand how <type of person or business> deals with <topic>. Not selling anything. Can I ask about the last time it came up?"

**Questions:**
1. Tell me about the last time <problem> happened. What did you do?
2. What made it a problem? What did it cost you (time, money, stress)?
3. What have you tried to solve it? What did you pay for that?
4. What didn't you like about those solutions?
5. Who else is involved in deciding about this?
6. If you could wave a wand, what would be different?
7. Who else should I talk to about this?

**Closing (only if there is an offer):** "We're running a small pilot for <outcome>, at <price>. Would you want in?" A real yes involves time, money or an introduction.

## Logging

After each conversation, write five lines in the dossier under `conversations` (role or segment, never full names):
- Trigger and last occurrence
- Cost of the problem
- Current solution and spend
- Exact phrases (verbatim) → also into `voice_of_customer`
- Commitment given: none, time, introduction, money

## Reading the pattern

After five conversations: does the same problem with the same trigger come back? Do people already spend money on it? If yes, the market and problem pillars score higher. If answers scatter, the customer pillar is the weak spot: narrow the segment and talk to five more.
