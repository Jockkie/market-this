# The ten go-to-market pillars

Score each pillar 0 to 10 with evidence. `seen` means the user said or showed it in this conversation; never score from a page, pitch or account you have not seen. The chain runs in this order; a weak early pillar weakens everything after it, so on a tie the earlier pillar wins.

| # | Pillar | Question it answers | 0 to 3 (weak) | 4 to 6 (moderate) | 7 to 10 (strong) |
|---|--------|---------------------|---------------|-------------------|------------------|
| 1 | Market | Is there enough demand that can be reached? | No evidence anyone pays for this | Some demand, unclear size or access | Clear demand, reachable segment, people already pay for alternatives |
| 2 | Customer (ICP) | Who exactly is the best-fit customer? | "Everyone" | A broad group | A specific segment with a trigger, budget and decision maker |
| 3 | Problem | Is the problem urgent and expensive enough? | Nice to have | Real but postponable | Urgent, costly, and they already try to solve it |
| 4 | Positioning | Why you instead of the alternatives? | Same as everyone | Some difference, not provable | Unique attributes, proven, tied to what the best customers value |
| 5 | Offer | Is it easy to say yes to? | Unclear what you get | Clear, but risky or complex to buy | Clear outcome, clear scope, low risk (guarantee, trial, small first step) |
| 6 | Pricing | Does the price fit value and buyer? | Guessed, or below cost | Market average, no logic | Based on value, tested, with a clear entry point |
| 7 | Motion | Is the way of selling right for the price and buyer? | Mismatch (high price, no sales contact; low price, heavy sales) | Works but costly | Fits price, buyer and team capacity |
| 8 | Channels | Do the chosen channels reach the ICP at a sane cost? | Random or none | One channel, untested | Tested channel with known cost per customer |
| 9 | Message | Does the copy say the promise in the customer's words? | Feature list, jargon | Clear but generic | Specific promise, customer language, one call to action |
| 10 | Proof | Is there enough trust to buy? | Nothing | Some testimonials | Results, cases, numbers, recognisable names, placed where doubt arises |

## Stage

- **Pre-launch:** no paying customers. Focus on pillars 1 to 5 and on ten direct conversations.
- **First customers:** 1 to 20 customers. Focus on ICP, offer and one channel.
- **Repeatable:** a channel works. Focus on conversion, pricing and proof.
- **Scaling:** focus on a second channel, partners, retention and referral.

## Playbook per pillar

**1. Market.** Check whether people already pay for alternatives (search volume, competitor revenue signals, marketplaces, job posts). If not: narrow to a niche where they do, or run a pre-sale or waiting list as a test before building more. Optional: MiroFish simulation of segment reactions.

**2. Customer.** Take the three best customers. List what they share: trigger, size, role of the buyer, budget, where they hang out. Write the ICP as: "<role> at <type of company or person> who <trigger>, and who now <old solution>." Name who is explicitly not a fit.

**3. Problem.** Rank problems by urgency and cost. Use the customer's words. If the problem is not urgent, attach it to a trigger moment (season, deadline, regulation, growth step).

**4. Positioning.** Follow `templates/positioning.md`: alternatives, unique attributes, value, best-fit customer, category. Choose a category that makes your strengths obvious. Test it in three conversations.

**5. Offer.** Make the outcome concrete, define the scope, add a small first step (paid pilot, audit, starter package), and reduce risk (guarantee, clear exit). Remove options that cause doubt.

**6. Pricing.** Price from the value of the outcome, not from hours. Offer three tiers when buyers differ in need. Test: raise the price for the next five prospects and watch the close rate. Optional: MiroFish simulation of price reactions.

**7. Motion.** Low price and simple product: product-led or self-serve. High price or complex decision: sales-led with conversations. Strong community or audience: community-led. Others already serve your ICP: partner-led. Match the motion to the hours available.

**8. Channels.** See `channels.md`. Bullseye, two channels, cheap tests, measure cost per customer.

**9. Message.** Promise, three pillars, proof per pillar, one call to action. Use verbatim customer phrases. For specific texts, hand off to Copywrite Me.

**10. Proof.** Collect results after every delivery: before and after, numbers, a quote with permission. Place proof right where doubt arises (near the price, near the call to action). Never invent or embellish proof.
